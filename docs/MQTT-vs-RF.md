# MQTT traffic vs. actual RF reception

Radio Gremlin's main job is to tell you **what your radio actually heard over the air**. A
Meshtastic node with an internet connection can also carry traffic from MQTT, and that traffic is
easy to mistake for RF.

## Two ways a packet reaches your node

**RF (LoRa).** Another radio transmitted it, and your radio received it over the air. These packets
carry a receive signal strength (RSSI, negative dBm) and a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) measured by
*your* radio. If a packet's hop count is unchanged (`hop_start == hop_limit`), it came directly from
the sender. Otherwise one or more radios relayed it.

**MQTT (internet).** Meshtastic nodes can bridge mesh traffic to an MQTT broker. A node with MQTT
**uplink** enabled publishes what it hears to the broker. A node with **downlink** enabled receives
packets from the broker. Through MQTT you can see nodes hundreds or thousands of kilometers away that
your radio could never hear. Those packets have no meaningful RSSI or SNR from your radio, and they
say nothing about your coverage.

## How Radio Gremlin classifies packets

The Mesh Command API labels each packet (see `packet_route()` in `gremlin/mesh_command.py`):

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `MQTT` | the packet is flagged `viaMqtt`, or its transport mechanism is MQTT |
| `GATEWAY` | it arrived over a UDP transport |
| `DIRECT` | received over RF (negative RSSI) with 0 hops used |
| `RELAYED` | received over RF after one or more hops |
| `RF UNKNOWN` | received over RF, but hop information is missing |
| `UNKNOWN` | none of the above, e.g. packets generated by your own node |

Mesh Observatory keeps these separate. **RF Watch**, the RF node directory and RF Signal Analysis
use only RF packets. The overall totals, the node directory ("MQTT ONLY" / "ALL NODES") and Mesh
Radar can include MQTT. In the author's first test run, most of the 1,100+ packets and 540+ node IDs
arrived via MQTT, and only three node IDs were heard over RF.

## The bot does not answer MQTT text messages

Meshing Around is an interactive bot: it answers commands and messages. Radio Gremlin patches it
(`meshing-around-patch/radio-gremlin.patch`) so that **text messages that arrived via MQTT are logged
and ignored**, with no bot reply. The goal is to observe the wider network without becoming noise on
it.

## What the patch does NOT change: firmware forwarding

The no-reply rule is in the bot software. It does not change what the **radio firmware** does:

- With MQTT **downlink** enabled on a channel, the node can take messages that arrived from the
  broker and transmit them over LoRa into your local mesh. That happens in the firmware whether or
  not the bot replies.
- With MQTT **uplink** enabled, anything your node hears or sends on that channel, including the
  bot's own RF replies, can be published to the broker.

If you want a node that observes MQTT without injecting internet traffic into your local RF mesh,
review your node's **MQTT module** and each **channel's uplink and downlink** settings with the
Meshtastic app or CLI. Respect your local mesh community's expectations. See the Meshtastic
documentation on MQTT for the current behavior of your firmware version: https://meshtastic.org/docs/software/integrations/mqtt/

## Reading the numbers

- A large **NODES** or **MQTT** count is not your RF coverage.
- **RF NODE IDs**, **RF PACKETS**, **DIRECT** and **RELAYED** in RF Watch are what your antenna
  actually did.
- Mesh Radar's "RF only" filter shows only nodes your radio heard over the air that also have a
  saved position.
